## What review is needed now

This round is for substantive and structural editing, not final typesetting.
The manuscript is full-volume and readable, but still contains author-owned
metadata placeholders. The editor should focus on book quality:

- reader promise and payoff;
- argument continuity across parts;
- repeated patterns that are helpful vs mechanical;
- overloaded late-book chapters;
- clarity of the book/companion boundary;
- terminology consistency for agent architecture, safety and operations.

## Main editorial questions

### Reader arc

Does the book move cleanly from demo-agent failure to platform responsibility,
then to safety, memory, runtime, evidence, operating model and launch?

### Audience calibration

Does the text speak to architects, technical leads, platform engineers,
security engineers and engineering leaders without turning into either a
beginner tutorial or an internal reference manual?

### Technical density

Where should a detailed schema, CLI path, YAML example or validation catalog
stay in the book, and where should it be routed to the online companion?

### Repetition

Which repeated chapter endings help the reader retain a working checklist, and
which ones feel like leftover web-page structure?

### Terminology

Are English terms kept only where they are stable engineering names or lookup
terms: capability, runtime, trace, policy gateway, tool gateway, rollout,
verifier, MCP, ADLC?

### Author voice

Where does the prose still read like assembled documentation rather than a
coherent author-led IT book?
